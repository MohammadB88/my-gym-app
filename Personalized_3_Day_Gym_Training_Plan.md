# Personalized Three-Day Gym Training Plan

**Training frequency:** 3 non-consecutive gym sessions per week  
**Session duration:** 60–90 minutes  
**Training effort:** Finish most working sets with approximately **2–3 clean repetitions in reserve**  
**Cardio:** Low-impact only  
**Primary approach:** Machine-focused, beginner-friendly, full-body training with knee-conscious exercise selection

> This plan is educational and does not replace individualized medical or physiotherapy advice. Because of the previous ACL reconstruction and bilateral meniscus history, personal symptom response, machine setup, and pain-free range of motion take priority over generic targets.

---

# Day 1 — Balanced Full-Body Training

**Focus:** Machine-focused, knee-conscious, upper/lower balanced, beginner-friendly  
**Balance:** 14 direct upper-body sets, 11–12 direct lower-body sets, and 2–3 core sets  
**Push/pull balance:** Approximately 7 push-focused and 7 pull-focused sets

## Warm-up

- Perform **5–8 minutes** of easy stationary or recumbent cycling.
- Complete **1 light practice set** before the first working set of:
  - Neutral-grip lat pulldown
  - Chest press
  - Hip thrust
  - Lying leg curl

## Workout

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Rest | Technique / safety cue | Primary focus |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Neutral-Grip Lat Pulldown Machine | 3 × 8–12 | 90 sec | Pull toward the upper chest. Keep the ribs controlled, avoid excessive backward lean, and do not pull behind the neck. | Upper back, lats, biceps |
| 2 | Machine Chest Press or Flat Bench Press | 3 × 8–10 | 90 sec | Set the handles near mid-chest. Keep the shoulder blades gently set and stop before pressing form changes. | Chest, triceps, front delts |
| 3 | Hip Thrust or Glute Bridge Machine | 3 × 8–12 | 90–120 sec | Drive through the feet and finish with the glutes. Avoid excessive lower-back arching. | Glutes, hamstrings |
| 4 | Lying Leg Curl | 3 × 10–12 | 75–90 sec | Align the knee with the machine pivot. Curl smoothly without lifting the hips from the pad. | Hamstrings, knee support |
| 5 | Reverse Pec Deck / Rear-Delt Machine | 2 × 12–15 | 60–75 sec | Keep the chest against the pad. Move the upper arms backward without shrugging or forcing the range. | Rear delts, rhomboids, middle trapezius |
| 6 | Seated Machine Overhead Press | 2 × 8–10 | 90 sec | Use a comfortable grip and range. Keep the back supported and avoid forcefully locking the elbows. | Shoulders, triceps |
| 7 | Seated Leg Extension | 2 × 12–15 | 60–75 sec | Begin with a light load and use only a pain-free, controlled range. Do not swing the weight or force full extension. | Quadriceps |
| 8 | Hip Abductor Machine | 2 × 12–15 | 60 sec | Keep the torso still and open the legs under control. Do not bounce at the end of the range. | Gluteus medius, outer hip |
| 9 | Hip Adductor Machine | 1–2 × 12–15 | 60 sec | Select a moderate, comfortable starting position and bring the legs together slowly. | Inner thigh, adductors |
| 10A | Machine Biceps Curl | 2 × 10–12 | Alternating pair | Align the elbows with the machine pivot. Avoid shoulder movement and forceful elbow lockout. | Biceps, brachialis |
| 10B | Machine Triceps Extension or Cable Pressdown | 2 × 10–12 | 60–75 sec after pair | Keep the elbows controlled and shoulders relaxed. Avoid torso momentum. | Triceps |
| 11 | Pallof Press or Dead Bug | 2–3 × 8–10 per side | 45–60 sec | Brace gently and keep the pelvis and rib cage controlled. Prioritize stability rather than load or speed. | Core stability, anti-rotation |

## How to run the session

- Perform exercises **10A and 10B as an alternating pair**:
  1. Complete one biceps-curl set.
  2. Complete one triceps set with only transition time between exercises.
  3. Rest for 60–75 seconds.
  4. Repeat for the second round.
- Choose loads that leave approximately **2–3 clean repetitions in reserve**.
- Stop a set when technique changes, even when repetitions remain.
- Knee exercises must stay pain-free and controlled. A shorter range and lighter load are acceptable starting points.

## Simple progression

- Keep the same load while building repetitions within the written range.
- When every working set reaches the top of its repetition range in **two consecutive Day 1 sessions** with good form and no meaningful joint reaction, increase the load by the smallest available increment.
- After increasing the load, return to the lower end of the repetition range and build upward again.
- For the first two weeks, keep the listed sets. Add volume only when recovery, sleep, and knee response are consistently good.
- Do not increase weight, repetitions, and sets at the same time.

## Optional cardio

- Perform **10–15 minutes** of easy-to-moderate stationary cycling after lifting.
- Use a pace at which you can speak in complete sentences.
- Reduce or skip cardio if the knees feel irritated.
- Swimming sessions count toward weekly cardiovascular work; avoid making every swim hard.

## Knee safety

- Stop any exercise that causes sharp pain, giving-way, locking, or a sudden increase in symptoms.
- If swelling or pain is meaningfully worse later that day or the next morning, reduce the next lower-body session’s load, range, or number of sets.
- Use only a comfortable, controlled range on the hip thrust, leg curl, leg extension, hip abductor, and hip adductor machines.
- Machine setup and exercise selection should be reviewed by a physiotherapist or qualified trainer when possible.

## Post-workout mobility — 5 to 8 minutes

- Standing calf stretch — 2 × 20–30 seconds per side
- Hip-flexor stretch — 2 × 20–30 seconds per side
- Gentle hamstring stretch — 2 × 20–30 seconds per side
- Doorway chest stretch — 2 × 20–30 seconds
- Thoracic rotation — 5 slow repetitions per side

# Day 2 — Horizontal Push/Pull + Posterior Chain

**Focus:** Machine-focused, back/posture emphasis, knee-conscious, beginner-friendly  
**Balance:** 16 direct upper-body sets, 11 direct lower-body sets, and 2–3 core sets  
**Push/pull balance:** Slightly pull-biased — approximately 9 pull-focused and 7 push-focused sets

## Warm-up

- Perform **5–8 minutes** of easy stationary or recumbent cycling.
- Complete **1 light practice set** before:
  - Chest-supported row
  - Incline machine chest press
  - Back extension
  - Seated leg curl

## Workout

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Rest | Technique / safety cue | Primary focus |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Chest-Supported Row Machine | 3 × 8–12 | 90 sec | Keep the chest on the pad. Pull the elbows back, pause briefly, and avoid shrugging. | Mid-back, lats, biceps |
| 2 | Incline Machine Chest Press | 3 × 8–10 | 90 sec | Set the handles around upper-chest level. Keep the shoulder blades stable and stop before form changes. | Upper chest, triceps, front delts |
| 3 | Back Extension Machine or 45° Bench | 3 × 10–12 | 75–90 sec | Use a small hip-driven arc. Finish tall without leaning backward or hyperextending. | Glutes, hamstrings, spinal erectors |
| 4 | Seated Leg Curl | 3 × 10–12 | 75–90 sec | Align the knees with the pivot. Curl smoothly and control the return without bouncing. | Hamstrings, knee support |
| 5 | Machine Pullover or Straight-Arm Pulldown | 2 × 10–12 | 60–75 sec | Keep the ribs controlled. Move through the shoulders without turning it into a triceps press. | Lats, serratus, upper back |
| 6 | Machine Lateral Raise | 2 × 12–15 | 60 sec | Raise only to a comfortable height. Keep the shoulders down and avoid swinging. | Side delts |
| 7 | Seated or Standing Calf Raise | 3 × 10–15 | 60–75 sec | Use a controlled stretch and rise. Keep pressure even across the forefoot. | Calves, ankle support |
| 8 | Hip Abductor Machine | 2 × 12–15 | 60 sec | Keep the torso supported and open the legs slowly. Do not bounce at the end of the range. | Gluteus medius, outer hip |
| 9 | Cable Face Pull | 2 × 12–15 | 60–75 sec | Pull toward eye level with the elbows high enough to stay comfortable. Do not arch the back. | Rear delts, upper back, rotator cuff |
| 10A | Machine Hammer Curl or Neutral-Grip Curl | 2 × 10–12 | Alternating pair | Keep the upper arms still and wrists neutral. Stop before the shoulders roll forward. | Biceps, brachialis, forearms |
| 10B | Rope Triceps Pressdown | 2 × 10–12 | 60–75 sec after pair | Keep the elbows close and shoulders relaxed. Use no torso momentum. | Triceps |
| 11 | Side Plank or Bird Dog | 2–3 sets per side | 45–60 sec | Brace gently and keep the trunk long. Choose the version that stays pain-free and controlled. | Core stability, anti-lateral flexion |

## How to run the session

- Perform exercises **10A and 10B as an alternating pair**:
  1. Complete one curl set.
  2. Complete one pressdown set.
  3. Rest for 60–75 seconds.
  4. Repeat for the second round.
- Keep approximately **2–3 clean repetitions in reserve**.
- Stop earlier if technique changes or a joint becomes irritated.
- Treat the back extension as a controlled hip-hinge substitute, not a maximum-load exercise.
- Use a short range and do not force the end position.

## Simple progression

- When every working set reaches the top of its repetition range in **two consecutive Day 2 sessions** with good form, increase the load by the smallest available increment.
- After increasing the load, return to the lower end of the repetition range and build upward again.
- For the first two weeks, keep the listed sets.
- Add no extra sets unless recovery, sleep, and next-day knee response remain consistently good.

## Optional cardio

- Perform **10–15 minutes** of easy-to-moderate stationary cycling after lifting when the knees feel calm.
- Use a conversational pace.
- Skip or shorten cardio if a swim is planned within 24 hours or the session is already near 90 minutes.
- No running or jumping.

## Knee safety

- Stop any movement that causes sharp pain, giving-way, locking, or a sudden unstable sensation.
- If pain or swelling is meaningfully worse later that day or the next morning, reduce the next lower-body session’s load, range, or number of sets.
- For the seated leg curl, align the knee with the pivot and avoid forcing the deepest bend.
- For calf raises, use support and a stable foot position.

## Substitutions / flare-up options

- Back extension → Glute bridge or hip-thrust isometric if the back or knees dislike the setup.
- Seated leg curl → Light standing band curl, or omit and reassess if posteromedial knee pain appears.
- Standing calf raise → Seated calf raise when balance or knee comfort is limited.

## Post-workout mobility — 5 to 8 minutes

- Standing calf stretch — 2 × 20–30 seconds per side
- Hip-flexor stretch — 2 × 20–30 seconds per side
- Gentle hamstring stretch — 2 × 20–30 seconds per side
- Doorway chest stretch — 2 × 20–30 seconds
- Thoracic extension or rotation — 5 slow repetitions per side

# Day 3 — Full-Body Strength + Control

**Focus:** Balanced push/pull, glute and quad control, low impact, beginner-friendly  
**Balance:** 14 direct upper-body sets, 12 direct lower-body sets, and 2–3 core sets  
**Push/pull balance:** Approximately 7 push-focused and 7 pull-focused sets

## Warm-up

- Perform **5–8 minutes** of easy stationary or recumbent cycling.
- Complete **1 light practice set** before:
  - High row
  - Seated shoulder press
  - Hip thrust
  - Seated leg extension

## Workout

| # | Exercise | Sets × reps | Rest | Technique / safety cue | Primary focus |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| 1 | High Row Machine | 3 × 8–12 | 90 sec | Keep the chest supported. Pull down and back without leaning or shrugging. | Upper back, lats, biceps |
| 2 | Seated Machine Shoulder Press | 3 × 8–10 | 90 sec | Use a comfortable grip and range. Keep the back supported and avoid hard elbow lockout. | Shoulders, triceps |
| 3 | Hip Thrust or Glute Bridge Machine | 3 × 10–12 | 90–120 sec | Drive through the feet and finish with the glutes. Keep the ribs down and avoid excessive back arching. | Glutes, hamstrings |
| 4 | Seated Leg Extension | 2 × 12–15 | 60–75 sec | Use a light load and pain-free range. Move slowly and do not kick or force lockout. | Quadriceps |
| 5 | Pec Deck or Cable Chest Fly | 2 × 10–12 | 60–75 sec | Use a comfortable shoulder range. Bring the arms together without rounding forward. | Chest, front delts |
| 6 | Neutral-Grip Seated Cable Row | 2 × 10–12 | 75–90 sec | Stay tall and pull toward the lower ribs. Avoid rocking backward. | Mid-back, lats, biceps |
| 7 | Seated Calf Raise | 3 × 12–15 | 60–75 sec | Pause briefly at the top and lower under control. Avoid bouncing. | Calves, ankle support |
| 8 | Supported Cable Hip Extension | 2 × 12–15 per side | 60 sec | Hold a stable support. Move from the hip without twisting the pelvis or arching the back. | Gluteus maximus, hip control |
| 9 | Hip Adductor Machine | 2 × 12–15 | 60 sec | Use a comfortable starting position and bring the legs together slowly. Do not force the stretch. | Adductors, inner thigh |
| 10A | Machine Biceps Curl | 2 × 10–12 | Alternating pair | Align the elbows with the pivot and keep the shoulders still. | Biceps, brachialis |
| 10B | Machine Triceps Extension or Cable Pressdown | 2 × 10–12 | 60–75 sec after pair | Keep the elbows controlled, shoulders relaxed, and torso still. | Triceps |
| 11 | Dead Bug | 2–3 × 6–10 per side | 45–60 sec | Keep the lower back gently supported and move the opposite arm and leg slowly. | Core stability, anti-extension |

## How to run the session

- Perform exercises **10A and 10B as an alternating pair**:
  1. Complete one biceps set.
  2. Complete one triceps set.
  3. Rest for 60–75 seconds.
  4. Repeat for the second round.
- Choose loads that leave approximately **2–3 clean repetitions in reserve**.
- The final repetitions should be challenging but controlled.
- The leg extension and cable hip extension must remain pain-free and stable.
- Shorter ranges are acceptable.

## Simple progression

- Use double progression: build repetitions first, then add the smallest available load only after every set reaches the top of its range twice with good form.
- After increasing the load, begin near the lower end of the repetition range.
- Every fifth or sixth week—or earlier when fatigue, sleep disruption, or knee irritation accumulates—reduce working sets by approximately one-third and stay farther from failure.

## Optional cardio

- Perform **8–15 minutes** of easy stationary cycling after lifting, or omit it when a swim is planned soon.
- Keep the pace conversational and prioritize smooth pedaling rather than resistance.
- No running, jumping, or impact intervals.

## Knee safety

- Stop for sharp pain, locking, giving-way, sudden instability, or significant swelling.
- For leg extension, begin light and use only a comfortable range; do not force the final degrees of extension.
- For the hip thrust and cable hip extension, use a stance and range where both knees feel stable.
- Reduce load or range if symptoms are worse later that day or the next morning.

## Substitutions / flare-up options

- Leg extension → Short-arc quad set or band terminal knee extension if the machine irritates the knee.
- Hip thrust machine → Floor glute bridge when the setup or knee angle is uncomfortable.
- Supported cable hip extension → Hip abductor machine or light standing hip extension without resistance.

## Post-workout mobility — 5 to 8 minutes

- Standing calf stretch — 2 × 20–30 seconds per side
- Hip-flexor stretch — 2 × 20–30 seconds per side
- Gentle quadriceps stretch — 1–2 × 20 seconds per side, only if comfortable
- Lat or doorway stretch — 2 × 20–30 seconds
- Thoracic rotation — 5 slow repetitions per side

# Weekly Structure and Recovery

A practical weekly structure is:

| Day | Training |
|---|---|
| Monday | Day 1 gym session |
| Tuesday | Easy swimming, walking, or recovery |
| Wednesday | Day 2 gym session |
| Thursday | Rest or easy swimming |
| Friday | Day 3 gym session |
| Saturday | Optional easy activity |
| Sunday | Rest |

The exact days can be changed, but keep the three gym sessions **non-consecutive whenever possible**.

## General progression rules

1. Start with a load that allows the bottom of the written repetition range with stable technique and no joint irritation.
2. Keep approximately **2–3 clean repetitions in reserve** on most working sets.
3. Build repetitions before adding weight.
4. Increase the load only after reaching the top of the range on every set for two sessions with good form.
5. Use the smallest practical weight increase.
6. After increasing the load, return to the lower end of the repetition range.
7. Do not increase load, repetitions, and total sets simultaneously.
8. Reduce training volume when sleep, stress, swimming load, or knee symptoms impair recovery.

## General knee-safety rules

- No running, jumping, or impact intervals.
- Use controlled repetitions and pain-free ranges.
- Stop for sharp pain, locking, giving-way, sudden instability, or significant swelling.
- Mild muscular effort is acceptable; joint pain that increases from repetition to repetition is not.
- If symptoms are meaningfully worse later that day or the next morning, reduce the next lower-body session’s load, range, or number of sets.
- Replace repeatedly painful exercises rather than forcing them.
- Seek assessment from a physiotherapist or qualified medical professional for persistent pain, swelling, locking, or instability.

## General cardio guidance

- Stationary or recumbent cycling is the preferred post-lifting option.
- Keep post-lifting cardio easy to moderate and conversational.
- Swimming contributes to weekly cardiovascular work.
- Avoid making all gym and swimming sessions hard.
- Shorten or omit post-lifting cardio when recovery is limited or a swimming session is planned within 24 hours.

## Use note

This plan is designed for a beginner returning to structured training with a history of ACL reconstruction and bilateral meniscus injuries. It prioritizes supported machine exercises, gradual progression, core stability, low-impact cardiovascular work, and long-term consistency. Individual medical or physiotherapy guidance takes priority over this document.
